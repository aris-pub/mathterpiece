Mathterpiece

A language for math you can touch.

  1. Declare what the figure is, does, and looks like.
  2. The compiler typechecks every possible state.
  3. The runtime makes it accessible and interactive.

 1; chords in a circle: from each point k to the point m × k
 2(int n 200 (const))
 3(real m 1 (>= 1) (<= 12))  ; typechecked: m stays in 1 to 12
 4(func spot ((real k)) (polar 1 (turn (/ k n))))
 5
 6; what the figure is
 7(scene chords
 8  (circle rim (r 1) (fill none) (stroke 'gray'))
 9  (foreach k n
10    (line_ (from (spot k)) (to (spot (* m k)))
11           ; what the figure looks like
12           (stroke (mix 'crimson' 'royalblue' (/ k n)))
13           (stroke-opacity 0.5))))
14
15; what it does
16(animation sweep (loop)
17  (move m (from 1) (to 12) (duration 40))
18  (move m (from 12) (to 1) (duration 40)))
19(canvas v chords (on-ready (animation sweep)))
20
21; accessible and interactive: mouse, touch or keyboard
22(slider times (move m (from 1) (to 12))
23  (label 'multiplier m'))
24(button again (label 'play') (animation sweep))

 1; the birthday problem: among 23 people a shared birthday is likely
 2(int n 23 (>= 2) (<= 60))  ; typechecked: 2 to 60 people
 3(func pair ((int m)) (- 1 (reduce (range m) 1
 4  (fn ((real q) (int k)) (* q (/ (- 365 k) 365))))))
 5(func yours ((int m)) (- 1 (^ (/ 364 365) (- m 1))))
 6
 7; what the figure is: both chances, for up to 60 people
 8(scene chance
 9  (line_ (from [0 15]) (to [60 15]) (stroke 'gray') (dash [4 4]))
10  (line_ (from [n 0]) (to [n 30]) (stroke 'gray'))
11  ; what the figure looks like
12  (polyline_ (stroke 'slateblue') (stroke-width 3)
13    (points (map (range 61) (fn ((int m)) [m (* 30 (pair m))]))))
14  (polyline_ (stroke 'crimson') (stroke-width 3)
15    (points (map (range 61) (fn ((int m)) [m (* 30 (yours m))]))))
16  (circle_ (r 1) (pos [n (* 30 (pair n))]) (fill 'slateblue'))
17  (circle_ (r 1) (pos [n (* 30 (yours n))]) (fill 'crimson')))

 1; Cauchy–Schwarz, as two rectangles of height ‖v‖
 2(point u [1.8 0.4]) (point v [0.5 1.4])
 3(real nu (dist [0 0] u))
 4(real nv (dist [0 0] v))
 5(point e (or? (* (/ 1 nv) v) [0 1]))  ; typechecked: v may be 0
 6(real s (+ (* u.x e.x) (* u.y e.y)))
 7(string lit '')
 8
 9(scene plane
10  (foreach k ['dot' 'bound']
11    (let ((w (* (if (= k 'dot') s (* (sign s) nu)) e))
12          (h (* nv (as point [e.y (- e.x)]))))
13      (polygon_ (points [[0 0] w (+ w h) h])
14        (fill (if (= k 'dot') 'slateblue' 'crimson'))
15        (opacity (if (= lit k) 0.45 0.12)))))
16  (line_ (from u) (to (* s e)) (stroke 'gray') (dash [4 4]))
17  (group arrows (defaults (stroke-width 3) (head-end 'dart'))
18    (line pu (from [0 0]) (to u) (stroke 'crimson'))
19    (line pv (from [0 0]) (to v) (stroke 'royalblue'))))
20(formula cs
21  tex'\Big|\underbrace{\term{dot}{\langle u, v\rangle}}_{\term{a}{}}\Big|
22      \term{rel}{} \underbrace{\term{bound}{\|u\|\,\|v\|}}_{\term{b}{}}'
23  (term a (tex $'{(abs (* s nv)):.2f}')) (term b (tex $'{(* nu nv):.2f}'))
24  (term rel (tex (if (< (* nv (- nu (abs s))) 0.005) '=' '\\le')))
25  (term dot (background (if (= lit 'dot') 'lavender' none)))
26  (term bound (background (if (= lit 'bound') 'lavender' none))))
27(canvas c (x -1.2) (y -1.5) (width 4) (height 3.8) plane
28  (on-drag pu (move u (to pointer))) (on-drag pv (move v (to pointer))))
29(display shown cs (foreach k ['dot' 'bound']
30  (on-enter #'cs.{k}' (set lit k)) (on-leave #'cs.{k}' (set lit ''))))

Accessible, interactive, portable by default

Every Mathterpiece figure gets these from the runtime, with no extra code.

  • KeyboardSliders, buttons and players work from the keyboard.
  • Screen readersEach control and each canvas carries a label written in the source.
  • TouchEvery drag also works by tapping.
  • Light and darkEach color has a dark value, and the page picks.
  • Reduced motionAnimations respect the reader's setting.
  • Saved as dataA figure compiles to a JSON file, so it keeps working when the runtime improves.

Typesafe figures

Mathterpiece figures never break in the reader’s browser: the compiler rejects any figure that could draw something undefined.

  1. 1Write the figure
    1(real k -1 (>= -1) (<= 1))
2(real r (sqrt (- k)))
3(scene parabola
4  (line_ (from [-2 0]) (to [2 0]) (stroke 'gray'))
5  (parametric p (for t -2 2) [t (+ (* t t) k)] (stroke 'teal'))
6  (circle lo (r 0.1) (pos [(- r) 0]) (fill 'crimson'))
7  (circle hi (r 0.1) (pos [r 0]) (fill 'crimson')))
8(canvas c parabola)
9(slider shift (move k (from -1) (to 1)) (label 'k'))
  2. 2The compiler rejects it
    $ mtp check roots.mtp
roots.mtp:2:9: r may be none: give it a default with (or? value default), or declare it real|none
$

When k is above 0, −k has no square root, so r has no value, and the two red dots would have no position. The error says what to change, so a person or a coding agent can fix the figure.

  1. 3Say what happens when there are no roots
     1(real k -1 (>= -1) (<= 1))
 2(real|none r (sqrt (- k)))
 3(scene parabola
 4  (line_ (from [-2 0]) (to [2 0]) (stroke 'gray'))
 5  (parametric p (for t -2 2) [t (+ (* t t) k)] (stroke 'teal'))
 6  (circle|none lo (r 0.1) (pos [(- r) 0]) (fill 'crimson'))
 7  (circle|none hi (r 0.1) (pos [r 0]) (fill 'crimson')))
 8(canvas c parabola)
 9(slider shift (move k (from -1) (to 1)) (label 'k'))
10(readout says (or? $'roots at ±{r:.2f}' 'no real roots'))
